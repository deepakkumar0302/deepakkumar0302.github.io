Working paper · 2-minute summary

Adoption of Agricultural Technologies by Smallholder Farmers

A synthesis of empirical evidence · Deepak Kumar and Miki Khanh Doan · World Bank Group working paper, 2025

Better seeds, fertilizer, irrigation and climate-smart practices exist, yet many poor farmers do not use them. Reviewing the evidence, we find that farmers face several barriers at once, so single fixes rarely work and well-designed bundles do better.

1 · Why it matters

Small farms feed much of the world

Roughly 510 million farms under two hectares produce about 35 percent of the world’s food. Raising their productivity and helping them cope with climate shocks is central to reducing poverty. We bring together evidence from randomized evaluations and large programs, mainly in Africa and Asia.

2 · The barriers

Four constraints, often at the same time

Money
credit and savings to pay for inputs up front
Risk
insurance against bad harvests and weather
Information
advice; in Sub-Saharan Africa one extension worker often serves more than 1,000 farmers
Markets
roads, buyers and fair prices for what they grow

Climate-smart practices face extra hurdles: their returns arrive slowly, some need neighbours to act together, and part of the benefit goes to society rather than the farmer. The barriers bite hardest for poorer, more remote and female farmers.

3 · Combining works

Two practices together beat either alone

4 · Bundling finance and risk

Insurance linked to credit changed behaviour in Ghana

5 · Bigger bundles

Graduation programs for the poorest

“Graduation” programs combine an asset, cash support, training, savings and coaching. They now run in more than 50 countries.

+7 to 65%

+11 to 30%

82%
of the poorest fifth in low-income countries receive no social assistance
Bundling works best when tailored to the full set of constraints poor farmers face. It adds complexity and cost, but offers greater potential to drive sustained adoption and resilience.
