Governance Response during COVID-19 and Political Affirmative Action

Evidence from local governments in India · with Vivek Pandey and Ankita Rathi

India reserves the leadership of some village councils for women. During COVID-19, these councils responded about as well as the rest. Women leaders did better where more women sat on the council and where the village economy was more diverse.

1 · The question

Do women leaders change how local government handles a crisis?

Much research argues that bringing women into public office changes institutions. COVID-19 put every village council to the same test at the same time. This paper asks whether councils led by a woman (a reserved Pradhan seat) responded differently, and under what conditions.

2 · The setting

Village councils in three Indian states

174
villages in Bihar, Madhya Pradesh and Gujarat
1,051
households surveyed before and after the 2020 lockdown
80
types of council COVID actions recorded
3 · How we measure it

A 0 to 100 score of each council’s response

Each council’s actions on communication, coordination, information and resource mobilization are combined into a Governance Response Index, from 0 (no response) to 100 (full response). Councils reserved for a woman leader are compared with unreserved ones.

4 · What happened

On average, no difference

Women-led councils’ overall response was not statistically different from that of other councils. Their response improved, however, where more women sat on the village council and where the village had a more diverse mix of businesses.

5 · Why it matters for households

More women in paid work

INR 736
higher monthly food spending (about USD 10) where women-led villages had more diverse businesses
Gendering politics through increasing female representation in local political institutions is a necessary but not sufficient condition for successful local governance.
